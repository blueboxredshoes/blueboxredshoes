I.

Whilst you’ve been away, I’ve forgotten you: I’ve forgotten the way your voice rings out and bursts into chimes and occasionally falters and the way you enter a room with light scattering from you and let it fall and fall with it and move between where it lands, between people in a room; the way you pipe up and pipe down, the way you chime in and between, the way you annoy me so with your teasing and slipping, the way you can never be found when needed for you’re always slipping away and between people and you can’t be held, grasped to feel the clutch to hold the thing, to be still.

I can’t remember you being still, except for maybe once when she was going to jump and you pulled her away and swallowed the lump of feeling, of difficulty, down and you dropped down and you were still then and you were swallowing then, and not speaking; I’ve forgotten the way you are when you can’t speak and have to stand staring and wait for something else to happen so you don’t have to stay still, locked on the thing; wait for someone else to move to try and hold you, and you accept it then and stand like a monument, like you’ve always been there, and then the moment passes and you slip away again, you peel away again and I don’t see you.

You peel away and I’ve forgotten the way you sometimes laugh like a hundred little bells pealing, your mouth feeling and bent to deploy the sound, blooming into petals (a little spurt here) (a little wheeze there) and falling, slowly floating, lingering on the soft and the full and a smile still plastered to one’s face, as if the muscles forgot to release in the bliss and the tender of the moment, and everything is ringing all at once, even for a long while after it’s stopped, and you’ve gone, and everything is stilled, but still ringing like how the room rings when a taboo is broken like breaking the silence to say something and everyone getting stuck at it and stunned by it for moments after and retreating very far inward to look for a step or a stile to hoist them over the thing as it lingers and spans to hit the wall and reverberates, fizzy and shaking and moving too much (fizzy and shaking and moving too much), not allowing itself to be forgotten, instead pasting the air with song, now bent steady and receiving the sound – and any interruption to your song is like the sound of a gum shoe sticking to the floor of a large hall, trailing the echo with the walk (trailing the echo) and getting stuck, muted by the adhesive, and the space is too grand and precious and quietly ringing with you and it’s funny then, and one can’t help but feel the lips curl upward.

I can’t remember the way your mouth moves to try and form the words when a conversation dries up and the room is expectant and waiting quiet, for it’s after dinner and you’ve exhausted the talk from everyone, and when you can’t so instead you heave and huff and hurl out the curd, the crust of a word, which is really just sighing noises that almost breach and break into, almost starting, only to retreat back into the quiet and then crack into laughter
and set the entire room off
until it's quietly ringing
